# ABSTRACT

Point cloud upsampling aims to generate a dense, well-distributed point set from a sparse input, and serves as a key intermediate stage in three-dimensional reconstruction and point cloud analysis pipelines. Recent learning-based methods have steadily improved fidelity metrics such as the Chamfer distance, and commonly introduce adversarial supervision to impose additional distribution-level constraints. However, existing work exhibits three shortcomings: the evidence linking a proposed modification to its claimed mechanism is rarely established at an intermediate level; inconsistent evaluation protocols make reported numbers difficult to compare across papers; and failure conditions together with negative results are seldom reported. Among the long-standing yet rarely examined practices is the use of a fixed constant as the weight of the adversarial loss. Whether such a constant continues to express the intended relative contribution throughout training has neither been measured nor tested under controlled comparison.

Taking this issue as the entry point, this thesis works under the 4x upsampling setting of the PU1K dataset. It first reproduces and verifies a joint baseline that combines a PU-Transformer generator with a PU-GAN style discriminator, then localises the bottleneck of that baseline using measured evidence, and finally proposes an improvement at the level of the training mechanism together with a controlled validation.

Baseline verification and bottleneck diagnosis yield three results. First, the generator implemented here has 1152803 parameters, a ratio of 1.1886 to the value reported in the original paper; a layer-by-layer check localises the discrepancy to the output projection of the multi-head self-attention, and three implementation details left unspecified in the original paper are explicitly annotated. Second, after calibrating the valid range of each metric with deliberately degenerate reference models, the blind spots of the metrics are found not to overlap, which makes joint multi-metric reporting a necessary condition. Third, the bottleneck is the dispersion of local spacing rather than a bias in overall density: the coefficient of variation of nearest-neighbour spacing in the output is 4.22 times that of the ground truth, without exception across all 200 samples, while the ratio of mean spacing is 0.916; the error increases monotonically with local input sparsity, the sparsest quartile being 1.476 times the densest. Critically, the rank correlation between the spacing coefficient of variation and the Chamfer distance is only $-0.217$, meaning that further reducing the fidelity error under pure Chamfer supervision does not improve uniformity. This directly motivates introducing auxiliary supervision and adopting spacing uniformity as the primary metric.

To address this bottleneck, this thesis proposes gradient-adaptive adversarial weighting (GAAW), and denotes the complete method adopting this mechanism as GA-PUT. The backbone architecture is left unchanged; the weight of the adversarial loss is adjusted online according to the ratio of gradient norms between the reconstruction branch and the adversarial branch, so that the relative contribution of the two branches is no longer fixed by a static constant. The positioning of this mechanism is stated explicitly: weighting by a gradient-norm ratio has a direct precedent in image generation, and this thesis **does not claim to be the first to propose the formula**. Its contribution is the adaptation, quantitative measurement and boundary delineation of that mechanism in the present setting.

Experiments are conducted on a validation subset of $n = 200$ under a single consistent protocol, and every verdict follows criteria fixed before the experiments were run. At the mechanism level, the ratio of gradient norms between the two branches spans a factor of $3.92×10^{6}$ over 150 epochs, and still $4.31×10^{5}$ after trimming the top and bottom 10% percentiles; the segment-wise medians increase strictly monotonically, and the fixed weight chosen by common practice deviates from the actual gradient environment in $91.3\%$ of the epochs. These measurements confirm that the mechanism is indeed triggered. At the level of effect, under identical machine, identical random seed and single-variable conditions, gradient-adaptive weighting improves the primary metric by $6.43\%$ relative to fixed weighting, an offset of 8.48 times the cross-sample standard error; per-sample pairing shows that 158 of the 200 samples ($79.00\%$) move in the favourable direction, indicating that the improvement occurs broadly across the sample population rather than being driven by a few extreme cases. The point estimates of the Chamfer distance and the Hausdorff distance also move in the favourable direction ($-1.36\%$ and $-1.69\%$), showing that the improvement is not obtained at the cost of geometric fidelity or worst-region risk; however, their offsets are only $0.32$ and $0.35$ standard errors, below the preregistered threshold, and therefore **no claim of effectiveness is made** for these two metrics.

Three results unfavourable to the proposed method are reported faithfully. First, introducing the adversarial branch itself degrades the primary metric under the present configuration: relative to the adversarial-free baseline, fixed weighting and adaptive weighting degrade it by $14.86\%$ and $7.47\%$ respectively, both significantly in the adverse direction; adaptive weighting therefore halves the degradation without turning it into an improvement. Accordingly the claim is strictly confined to "if adversarial training is used, gradient-adaptive weighting outperforms fixed weighting", and it is **not** claimed that using adversarial training outperforms not using it. Second, a simpler competing option offers a better cost-benefit ratio: an explicit uniformity constraint improves the primary metric by $5.44\%$ at only $13.04\%$ additional training cost, with no extra parameters and no discriminator, whereas the proposed method requires $70.58\%$ additional training time and 255426 discriminator parameters. Third, the benefit of the proposed method cannot be attributed between "dynamic adjustment" and "a smaller weight magnitude", because the control experiment of manually setting a smaller fixed weight was not performed. In addition, adjusting the weights of the bidirectional Chamfer terms cannot substitute for an explicit uniformity constraint, and the more aggressive the weighting the worse the degradation; combining two loss terms produces an adverse interaction; and adding the standard attention scaling to the reproduced backbone causes all three metrics to collapse simultaneously. These three failure cases jointly delineate the failure boundary of the method.

The confirmable contributions of this thesis are fourfold: a quantitative measurement of the reconstruction-adversarial gradient imbalance in this setting; the adaptation and controlled validation of gradient-norm-ratio weighting; a complete validation report that includes negative results, a competing option and training cost; and the full execution of preregistered criteria. All conclusions are confined to the PU1K dataset, the patch formulation, $N = 256$, $r = 4$ and a single random seed, and are not extrapolated across datasets, upsampling ratios or backbone networks. Robustness across random seeds was not measured, so the precise statement of the main conclusion is that it was "observed in this controlled run".

**Key Words:** point cloud upsampling; adversarial training; gradient scale imbalance; adaptive loss weighting; distribution uniformity; ablation study
