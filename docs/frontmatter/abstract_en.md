# ABSTRACT

Point cloud upsampling aims to generate a denser point set from sparse observations while preserving geometric fidelity and local distribution quality. Chamfer distance is widely used as a reconstruction objective, but it primarily measures nearest-neighbor proximity between two sets and does not, by itself, enforce regular spacing among generated points. Adversarial learning can provide distribution-level supervision, yet it introduces a second problem: how the reconstruction and adversarial objectives should be weighted throughout training. A fixed adversarial coefficient may correspond to different gradient contributions at different stages, while conclusions based on a single final metric may confuse an improvement in local spacing with an overall improvement in point-cloud quality.

This thesis studies 4× point cloud upsampling on PU1K using a PU-Transformer generator and a PU-GAN-style discriminator. It decomposes output quality into average geometric fidelity, worst-case error, local spacing dispersion, and sparse-region coverage, and establishes an evidence chain from configurations and code to checkpoints, per-sample measurements, aggregate statistics, and textual claims. Inspired by the adaptive discriminator weighting used in Taming Transformers/VQGAN, this work adapts the ratio of reconstruction-gradient and adversarial-gradient norms to point cloud upsampling and formulates Gradient-Adaptive Adversarial Weighting (GAAW). GAAW measures the Chamfer reconstruction gradient and generator adversarial gradient over a shared generator parameter scope, introduces a target gradient ratio and a linear ramp factor, and adjusts the generator-side adversarial coefficient for each batch. The gradient-norm ratio itself is not claimed as a novel formula; the contribution is limited to its point-cloud adaptation, parameter-scope choice, controlled comparison, and analysis of failure boundaries.

A split audit found that only 11 of the 200 archived diagnostic records intersect the true held-out validation split generated by the training code; the remaining 189 belong to the training partition. The former claim of “200 validation samples” is therefore withdrawn, and only an exploratory reanalysis of the 11-record intersection is reported. Relative to its non-adversarial baseline, the fixed coefficient 8.27 worsens the nearest-neighbor coefficient of variation, shared-ground-truth-frame diagnostic Chamfer distance, and sparse-stratum Q4/Q1 ratio by 14.67%, 6.15%, and 6.34%, while improving Hausdorff distance by 1.18%. Relative to this fixed coefficient, GAAW improves the nearest-neighbor coefficient of variation and Q4/Q1 by 4.84% and 2.24%, but worsens Chamfer and Hausdorff distances by 2.80% and 13.43%. Relative to the non-adversarial baseline, GAAW is worse on all four measures by 9.13%, 9.13%, 12.10%, and 3.96%, respectively. A direct uniformity loss improves the nearest-neighbor coefficient of variation and Hausdorff distance by 7.00% and 6.77%, while worsening Chamfer distance and Q4/Q1 by 1.07% and 18.92%. The available evidence therefore supports only the claim that GAAW mitigates local-spacing dispersion caused by this particular fixed coefficient; it does not support comprehensive geometric improvement or superiority over non-adversarial training.

Negative ablations involving directional Chamfer reweighting and combined constraints are retained rather than omitted. The attention-scaling measurement is excluded because the evaluation script reconstructed the wrong model configuration. The present evidence is limited to one training seed per configuration, and the 11-record intersection cannot represent the full validation set. Some original training artifacts and per-batch gradient traces are not fully archived, while formal test-set NUC, P2F, and qualitative point-cloud results remain to be completed. The results therefore support only a small-sample exploratory conclusion for fixed models, not cross-seed significance, generalization, or universal superiority. The principal value of this thesis is a falsifiable workflow that connects algorithmic motivation, gradient measurement, multi-metric evaluation, competitive baselines, invalid-measurement isolation, and negative results, thereby defining both the supported claims and the boundaries of GAAW.

**Key words:** point cloud upsampling; gradient-adaptive weighting; adversarial learning; Chamfer distance; local distribution; reproducible experiment
